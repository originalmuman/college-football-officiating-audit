### Entry 1 — Oct 15, 2005: Michigan 27, Penn State 25 ("the two seconds")
- **Date:** Oct 15, 2005 (Michigan Stadium, Ann Arbor)
- **Game/Conference:** Michigan at Penn State, Big Ten
- **What was contested:** With Penn State leading 25-21 in the final minute, Michigan coach Lloyd Carr argued the game clock had kept running after a Michigan timeout and demanded time be restored. Officials put **two seconds back on the clock**. With :01 showing, Chad Henne hit Mario Manningham for a 10-yard TD as time expired — walk-off 27-25 win. Contemporary accounts: "those two seconds the officials put back on the clock, two seconds that ultimately gave the Wolverines a remarkable victory" (Penn State game account). Paterno's response: "They gave them two seconds. They won the ball game. It's over."
- **Who benefited:** Michigan.
- **League response/admission:** No documented Big Ten admission of error for the clock restoration. The Big Ten's broader response was institutional: in later years (notably after 2014) the conference cited the 2005 game as part of the history driving replay-mechanism reforms. Result stood; PSU's first unbeaten-season bid since 1994 ended.
- **Sources:** frontiernet.net PSU game account; Pittsburgh Post-Gazette Chico Harlan Q&A ("the two seconds that were added back"); r/CFB Penn State wiki ("Michigan win with one second remaining after two seconds were added to the game clock").
- **RESULT CHANGED?** No.
### Entry 2 — Oct 25, 2014: Ohio State 31, Penn State 24 (2OT) — Big Ten admits two blown calls
- **Date:** Oct 25, 2014 (Beaver Stadium)
- **Game/Conference:** Ohio State at Penn State, Big Ten
- **What was contested:** (a) 1st quarter: Ohio State S Vonn Bell's "interception" clearly hit the ground on replays (pass intended for Matt Zanellato); upheld on the field because the replay booth had no access to all TV feeds at game start — later called a "technical difficulty." (b) 2nd quarter: Ohio State snapped the ball **several seconds after the play clock expired** before a 49-yard Sean Nuernberger field goal; no delay-of-game called.
- **Who benefited:** Ohio State (won 31-24 in 2OT; both errors directly contributed to Buckeye points in a one-score game).
- **League response/admission:** **Yes — Big Ten formally admitted both errors the following day (Sunday/Monday Oct 26-27, 2014).** Conference statement (to ESPN's Adam Rittenberg): the missed delay of game resulted from "a breakdown in officiating mechanics," and "technical issues" prevented the booth from properly reviewing the Bell interception. Head referee John O'Neill: "The play, technically, was not thoroughly reviewed due to some technical difficulties with the equipment... The feeds that the replay team looks at are the feeds that you get at home... We can't create our own rules." The Big Ten subsequently instated a "failproof replay mechanism" (per Big Ten coordinator of officials, admitting the mistakes again in an Aug 2015 media address). Result stood; no crew suspension publicly announced.
- **Sources:** CBS Sports ("Big Ten explains officiating mistakes"); SI (Oct 27, 2014, "Delay of game penalty missed on Ohio State field goal"); McCall/Times-Tribune ("Big Ten admits to officiating blunders"); Onward State (Aug 1, 2015: coordinator admits mistakes, failproof replay mechanism).
- **RESULT CHANGED?** No.

### Entry 4 — Oct 31, 2015: Miami 30, Duke 27 — 8-lateral kickoff return; ACC suspends crew, admits TD should not have counted
- **Date:** Oct 31, 2015 (Wallace Wade Stadium, Durham)
- **Game/Conference:** Miami at Duke, ACC
- **What was contested:** With 0:06 left and Duke ahead 27-24, Miami fielded the kickoff and executed **eight laterals** across the field, returning it ~91 yards (return ended by tackles short? No — Corn Elder took it in) for the game-winning TD as time expired. Replays showed: returner's knee down, a block in the back, and an illegal forward lateral.
- **Who benefited:** Miami (won 30-27 on a TD the league later said should not have counted).
- **League response/admission:** **Yes — the most severe documented penalty of this era.** The next day (Nov 1, 2015) the ACC: (a) acknowledged a "series of errors during the final play of the game" (USA Today/CBS: the league said the winning TD "should not have counted"); (b) suspended the entire on-field officiating crew, the replay official, and replay communicator for **two conference games**; (c) took "additional disciplinary actions" against two more officials. USA Today: "Though the ACC is not overturning the result of the game..." Duke spokesman Jon Jackson pointed to the ACC statement; AD Kevin White declined further comment. Coach David Cutcliffe said afterward there should be a process in place for results to be overturned in such circumstances (abc11). The task-context quote ("may have been filled with errors but it was also filled with...") could NOT be verified in any retrievable source — do not use without confirmation. Result stood.
- **Sources:** ESPN (ACC suspends officials); NCAA.com; USA Today; CBS Sports; Washington Post ("ACC suspends Miami-Duke officiating crew, but won't change result").
- **RESULT CHANGED?** No — despite explicit league admission the score was wrongly awarded.

### Entry 5 — Nov 7, 2015: Arkansas 53, Ole Miss 52 (OT) — fourth-and-25 lateral; SEC says crew got it right (anchor correction)
- **Date:** Nov 7, 2015 (Oxford)
- **Game/Conference:** Arkansas at No. 18 Ole Miss, SEC
- **What was contested:** OT, 4th-and-25, Arkansas trailing: Hunter Henry flung the ball backward ("Hog-and-ladder") to Alex Collins, who ran 31 yards for a first down; Arkansas scored and won on a 2-pt conversion. Fans alleged a forward lateral and missed facemask on the return.
- **Who benefited:** Arkansas.
- **League response/admission:** **None — the SEC defended the crew.** SEC supervisor of officials Steve Shaw (week of Nov 13, 2015): the lateral was "correctly ruled a backward pass" and the play was properly officiated — he even used it as a teaching tool for clinics ("I thought our guys handled it very well"). No suspension; no admission of error. **Note: the task's anchor ("2016: SEC suspended referee crew after Arkansas-Ole Miss") did NOT verify.** The actual documented SEC crew suspension connected to an Arkansas game is **Oct 2009**: Commissioner Mike Slive suspended referee Marc Curles' crew (for ~a month / removed from Oct 31 assignment) after controversial personal-foul calls in **Arkansas at Florida** (Oct 17, 2009 — personal foul on DL Malcolm Sheppard in the 4th quarter that helped Florida) — a crew that had also worked the LSU-Georgia game (A.J. Green celebration penalty). The SEC office publicly said the crew "made a mistake" on the Sheppard call. Result stood.
- **Sources:** WholeHogSports (Shaw); ESPN "Crew removed from Oct. 31 game"; Jacksonville.com; Fox Sports; wholehogsports 2009.
- **RESULT CHANGED?** No (2009 Florida-Arkansas 23-20 result stood as well).

### Entry 8 — Sept 16, 2006: Oregon 34, Oklahoma 33 — onside-kick replay error; Pac-10 suspends officials
- **Date:** Sept 16, 2006 (Autzen Stadium)
- **Game/Conference:** Oklahoma at Oregon, non-conference (Pac-10 officiating crew)
- **What was contested:** Trailing 33-27 late, Oregon recovered an onside kick that television replays showed was touched by an Oregon player before traveling 10 yards — possession should have gone to Oklahoma. Replay officials upheld Oregon's recovery; on the next play Oregon scored the winning TD on a pass-interference-heavy snap in the end zone.
- **Who benefited:** Oregon.
- **League response/admission:** **Yes.** The Pac-10 suspended the replay crew for errors that "cost Oklahoma" (ESPN: "Pac-10 suspends officials for errors that cost Oklahoma"); officials on the field gave the ball to Oregon and replay did not overturn it. Then-OU coach Bob Stoops and AD Joe Castiglione publicly blasted the officiating; Oklahoma sought a result change and was refused. Result stood.
- **Sources:** ESPN (Pac-10 suspends officials).
- **RESULT CHANGED?** No.
