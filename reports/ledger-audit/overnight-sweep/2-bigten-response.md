**Yes, the Big Ten issued a statement shortly after the game (posted on their official X account around 4:48 AM UTC on September 6, 2026).** They stood by the replay officials’ decision to add one second back to the clock. The statement explained that Western Michigan’s #18 (Micah Davis) started his jump from an established out-of-bounds position (left foot) and made contact with the ball with one second remaining, per video review using a direct shot of the stadium game clock. It noted that replay cameras (including the clock camera) are synced via strobe 85 minutes before kickoff and cited NCAA Football Rule 4-2-3-a (ball is out of bounds—and clock stops—when it touches a player who is out of bounds). They included a video of the replay process.[[1]](https://x.com/RJ_cfb/status/2096462011953549428)

This came after initial reports that the conference planned to comment, and it was released in the hours following the Saturday night (Sept. 5) game. There is no indication of a *further* statement specifically “coming Sunday”—this was the official response. The MAC commissioner (Jon Steinbrecher) later issued a brief acknowledgment of the Big Ten’s explanation.[[2]](https://x.com/camijustice/status/2096465828346499211)

**Reporters and analysts overwhelmingly say the result cannot and will not be changed.** NCAA rules state that once the referee declares the game ended, the score is final; there is no mechanism to overturn or reverse the outcome for an officiating/clock error after that point (even if later deemed incorrect). Western Michigan’s president (Russ Kavalhuna) and coach (Lance Taylor) said they would discuss it with the MAC commissioner and seek any possible review, but they and others acknowledged it was unlikely to alter anything. The game stands as a 13-12 Michigan win. Some coverage notes this is consistent with past controversial finishes where results were not reversed.[[3]](https://heavy.com/sports/college-football/michigan-wolverines/ncaa-overturn-controversial-michigan-win-western-michigan/)

NBC rules analyst Terry McAulay (and others) publicly questioned the call based on broadcast footage showing the clock at 0:00 before the ball was touched, but the Big Ten’s statement addressed the discrepancy by pointing to their synced stadium-clock footage and the out-of-bounds contact. Public/X reaction included accusations of a “home” or conference-favoring call, but no reporting suggests the NCAA or Big Ten will vacate or reverse it.
