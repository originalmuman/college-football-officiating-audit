**Michigan escaped with a 13-12 win over Western Michigan on Sept. 5, 2026, via a controversial second-chance Hail Mary (Bryce Underwood to JJ Buchanan) after officials restored 1 second following an incomplete previous Hail Mary that TV clocks showed as time expired.** The Broncos led 12-7 (user note of 12-6 is close; last FG made it 12-7 with ~35 seconds left) and appeared to have a historic upset before the review and TD. Michigan was a massive favorite in Kyle Whittingham’s debut.[[1]](https://apnews.com/article/michigan-western-michigan-score-95cd312e290a27cb72453f4d09a8a210)

**Closing lines (pregame consensus around kickoff):** Michigan -27.5 (some books -28 or -28.5), over/under 49.5 (opened lower, around 47.5-48.5, and moved up). Moneyline: Michigan roughly -4000 to -8000/-10000 (e.g., -5000, -5556, -6500), Western Michigan +1600 to +2500/+1967. Sources include BetMGM, FanDuel, DraftKings, and others. The final 13-12 (total 25 points) meant Michigan did **not** cover the spread and the under cashed easily.[[2]](https://www.cbssports.com/betting/news/michigan-western-michigan-prediction-odds-time-2026-week-1-college-football-picks-bets-bets/)

**Betting impact of the final play:** The clock-zero first Hail Mary (incomplete, appearing to end the game with WMU winning) would have made Western Michigan the winner. The restored second and successful Hail Mary flipped the moneyline result. Michigan was such a heavy favorite that the vast majority of moneyline action was on the Wolverines. A WMU win would have forced sportsbooks to pay long-shot underdog tickets (potentially costly given the odds). One post claimed Vegas sportsbooks “saved millions” and it would have been one of the costliest regular-season CFB days if the upset stood; this is unverified by official reports. A VegasInsider reporter noted DraftKings had 79% of spread money on Michigan and BetMGM 76%. Since Michigan failed to cover, books likely profited on the spread as well. Live betting and in-game WMU moneyline/parlays would have been hit hardest by the flip. No major sportsbook statements (DraftKings, FanDuel, BetMGM, etc.) detailing exact handle, liabilities, or losses/gains were found in searches. The game’s low total and non-cover aligned with public betting lean (heavy on Michigan spread).[[3]](https://x.com/PatrickE_Vegas/status/2096438017691111590)

**Bettor reactions/outrage on X:** Posts mixed celebration (Michigan fans) with widespread “rigged,” “robbed,” “cheated,” and “NCAA needs investigation” comments, especially from neutrals or WMU supporters. Examples include pain for anyone on WMU ML (+2000-ish) and claims the first play ended the game. Engagement was modest on most betting-specific posts (views in the dozens to low thousands); the official @UMichFootball “1-0” post had ~725k views. No blockbuster viral betting-loss threads with massive reach numbers stood out beyond general controversy discussion. Some tied it to “Vegas” or refs, though others noted WMU still had to defend the Hail Mary.[[4]](https://x.com/backdoorisopen/status/2096436108540432700)

Overall, the play primarily flipped moneyline outcomes (and live bets) from a potential massive underdog cash to the expected favorite win, while the spread and total results were already unfavorable for Michigan backers. Searches turned up recaps and odds more than precise financial impact data.
