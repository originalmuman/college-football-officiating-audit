**The 2006 Oregon-Oklahoma game (34-33 Oregon win) is the most infamous and well-documented case of Oregon benefiting from contested officiating.** Pac-10 officials awarded Oregon possession on a late onside kick despite clear video evidence of illegal touching (Oregon player contacted the ball before 10 yards) and Oklahoma’s Allen Patrick recovering it uncontested and holding it up. Replay official Gordon Riese (an Oregon resident) did not overturn it, citing limited angles and technical issues; a subsequent PI call on Oklahoma also stood. Oregon scored the winning TD. The Pac-10 publicly apologized, suspended the crew (including Riese) for one game, and admitted the errors altered the outcome. Bob Stoops and Oklahoma president David Boren called it an “outrageous injustice.” It ranks among college football’s worst calls ever.[[1]](https://www.si.com/college/oklahoma/football/oklahoma-vs-oregon-2006-allen-patrick-recovered-the-football)

**The 2022 Oregon-Washington State game (44-41 Oregon win) involved a down-indicator error that skipped second down for WSU after an intentional grounding penalty (loss of down) on first down.** Officials jumped to third down; WSU ran and punted. After review (permitted by rule), they replayed third-and-long instead of restoring second down. The Pac-12 acknowledged the “embarrassing” human/procedural error, said protocols were followed to correct it, and reviewed for discipline. WSU fans and analysts (including John Canzano) noted it disrupted flow and was part of WSU’s pattern of officiating misfortune in the Pac-12. It did not fully restore the original series.[[2]](https://www.oregonlive.com/ducks/2022/09/pac-12-acknowledges-erroneous-down-in-oregon-washington-state-game-says-proper-protocols-followed-to-correct-error.html)

**The “2025 Fiesta Bowl Miami PI no-call vs. Ole Miss” is not an Oregon game.** It was the Jan. 2026 CFP semifinal (Miami 31-27 over Ole Miss). A Hail Mary incomplete pass had apparent jersey-grabbing by Miami’s Ethan O’Connor on Ole Miss’ De’Zhaun Stribling; officials cited “mutual combat” and no-called it. Analysts (Bill LeMonnier) backed the no-call as typical for Hail Marys; others (Lane Kiffin, some fans) disagreed. Oregon played Indiana in the Peach Bowl around the same time (lost 56-22) and had separate complaints. This appears to be a mix-up of CFP games.[[3]](https://www.sportingnews.com/ca/ncaa-football/news/mutual-combat-final-play-miami-ole-miss-pass-interference/fce3247fed036c9041f6ed0e)

**In the Sept. 5, 2026 Oregon-Boise State game (34-27 Oregon win), missed calls were mixed and debated immediately.** CBS rules analyst Gene Steratore said a missed kick-catch interference (halo/fair-catch violation) on Oregon’s muffed punt allowed Boise State to recover and score a TD on the next play—this *hurt* Oregon. Later, a no-call on Oregon DE Nasir Wyatt hitting Boise State QB Maddux Madsen in the facemask/roughing the passer on a key third down (Steratore said it should have been flagged) helped Oregon by stalling a Boise State drive. Other PI/no-call debates went both ways; Oregon had more penalties. Boise State fans and some posts claimed missed PIs and the RTP no-call protected Oregon; Oregon fans and others noted the KCI and overall flags favored Boise State. It was a close, penalty-filled game with typical Week 1 scrutiny.[[4]](https://www.si.com/college/oregon/football/oregon-ducks-suffer-pivotal-missed-call-boise-state-broncos)

**Other frequently cited cases where fans (mostly opponents) say Oregon benefited:**
- **2025 vs. Penn State**: Overturned fumble on Oregon RB Noah Whittington (knee down?) inside the red zone; Oregon scored a TD on the next play and won in OT. Ranked among 2025’s worst calls by On3; James Franklin and Penn State fans called it lopsided. Oregon also had a missed targeting complaint in OT.[[5]](https://www.cbssports.com/college-football/news/oregon-gets-fumble-ruling-overturned-against-penn-state-after-controversial-call-leads-to-td-for-the-ducks/)
- **2024 vs. Ohio State**: Oregon’s 12-man (illegal substitution) penalty at the end of a close win ran the clock (4 seconds elapsed); Ohio State couldn’t get another play. Rules were later changed to allow clock reset. Lanning’s gamesmanship, not a blown call, but opponents called it a loophole/refs not penalizing “unfair act.”
- **2024 vs. Michigan**: Questionable TD catches/reviews for Oregon (possible bounce, control issues) that stood.
- Pac-12-era examples include various PI, targeting, and downs issues (Oregon often involved as a high-profile team). Pac-12 officiating had a documented poor reputation (scandals like 2018 third-party override, frequent errors), which amplified perceptions.

**There is a running fan narrative among rival fanbases (Oklahoma, Washington State, Penn State, Ohio State, Michigan, Iowa, Indiana, Boise State) that Oregon benefits from favorable/home/Pac-12/Big Ten officiating, “home cooking” at Autzen, or inconsistent refs that swing close games.** It is not unique—CFB fans routinely blame refs after losses, and Pac-12’s historical issues (apologies, suspensions, command-center problems) fed it more than a specific Oregon conspiracy. Oregon fans counter with examples of bad calls against them. No evidence of systemic favoritism beyond typical high-profile-team scrutiny and conference officiating quality. The 2006 game is the cornerstone; recent Big Ten games (Penn State B1G title, Iowa, Michigan) keep it alive.
