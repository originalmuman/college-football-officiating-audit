**No one is publicly building a comprehensive, ongoing database or longitudinal study that fully matches the criteria** (multi-year team penalty differentials + referee crew ATS records + public league error admissions). Officiating data in CFB is fragmented, often conference-controlled or proprietary, and far less centralized/public than NBA referee tracking (e.g., Covers-style crew stats). Play-by-play sources enable custom analyses, but dedicated public trackers are limited.[[1]](https://collegefootballdata.com/about)

### Closest Academic Work
The most rigorous longitudinal statistical studies come from **Rhett Brymer** (Miami University) and collaborators (including students Mickey Whitford and Michael Macey, plus Ryan Rodenberg and others). They analyzed FBS penalty data across 2005–2015:

- 2005–2012 games (Sloan Sports Analytics Conference paper): Contrasted in-conference vs. out-of-conference games as a natural experiment. Found biases tied to betting lines (e.g., ACC/former Big East officials favoring underdogs in-conference), home-field (ACC/Big 12), and game pace. Some biases were revenue-inhibiting for conferences.[[2]](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/303326028_Referee_Analytics_Bias_in_Major_College_Football_Officiating)
- 2012–2015 follow-up (~38,000–39,000 penalty observations from SportSource Analytics, including declined/offsetting flags): ~10% fewer discretionary flags (holding, PI, unsportsmanlike, personal fouls) on “protected flagship” teams (high-revenue/playoff-contending programs with winning traditions). Strongest in the Big Ten. Variations by conference, year, home team, and betting line. Officials (conference employees) showed partiality toward high-revenue teams. Paper: “Exploring Discretionary Biases in Major College Football.” A 2021 *Eastern Economic Journal* article extended this to sports betting impacts.[[3]](https://www.miamialum.org/?pgid=13325&gid=1&cid=26353)

These used Excel/Stata modeling, controlled for closeness, prestige, etc. They are the closest to a multi-year bias study but are one-off academic papers, not an updating public database. Other papers exist on related topics (e.g., more penalties for teams with Black coaches, 2014–2019; HBCU vs. PWI penalty disparities).

### Closest Databases/Dashboards and Fan/Data Projects
- **Championship Analytics Inc. (CAI) Penalty Analysis Dashboard**: The nearest commercial/public-facing tool. It covers 2020–2024 (updating) FBS seasons, all teams, play types (run/pass/kick/punt), specific officials (by position: referee, umpire, etc.), crews, and penalty categories vs. NCAA averages. Users can compare two teams’ offensive/defensive rates, filter by official/crew, and see game-level crew tables. Aimed at coaches/analysts (clients include multiple P4 programs); a dashboard/view and user manual are available on their site. Not fully open/free like a public GitHub DB, but the most structured crew/referee tendency tracker.[[4]](https://www.championshipanalytics.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/CAI-Penalty-Analysis-Dashboard-User-Manual.pdf)

- **Fan/data journalism**: Addicted To Quack (ECoop, Dec 2025) analyzed judgment-call penalties (holding, PI, facemask, unsportsmanlike, roughing, etc.) by officiating *conference* (as crew proxy) in P4 regular-season games, 2020+. Big Ten crews call far fewer holdings (half the SEC/ACC rate recently); differences in DPI, OPI, defensive holding, etc. Uses play-by-play data; not crew-specific or ATS-focused.[[5]](https://www.addictedtoquack.com/general-college-football/56523/football-penalty-prevalence) A 2025 Reddit r/CFBAnalysis post shared a ChatGPT-assisted dashboard from CFBD data for weekly penalty analysis (fan-built, shareable scripts).

- **Betting-oriented**: A 2019 Sports Handle piece examined anonymized officials’ games for ATS, unders, home-favorite biases, and penalty rates (e.g., certain crews correlating with unders). Not a public ongoing tracker or named-crew database. No Covers.com equivalent exists for CFB referee crews. Betting sites/models (e.g., using CFBD API) incorporate penalties/spreads but not dedicated crew ATS records.[[6]](https://sportshandle.com/college-football-officiating-trends-betting/)

### Data Infrastructure Enabling Analyses
CollegeFootballData.com (CFBD) and cfbfastR (R package wrapping it + ESPN/NCAA sources) provide play-by-play (penalties included, 2001+ or 2014+), box scores, betting lines, and some officials data (especially NCAA loaders). This lets anyone build custom penalty/crew analyses (as fans have). SportSource Analytics supplied Brymer’s data and powers other stats sites. Internal tools like QwikRef exist for conference evaluations but are not public.[[7]](https://cfbfastr.sportsdataverse.org/)

### League Admissions of Errors
Conferences (not a central NCAA office) assign/evaluate crews. Evaluations use systems like QwikRef (play grading, including no-calls); results and punishments are generally private. NCAA has done officiating reviews (e.g., 2020 report) and tracking (targeting rates declining). Historical public actions (e.g., 2009 SEC crew suspension) exist, but no public longitudinal error database or admissions tracker. High-profile complaints (e.g., targeting, specific games) get media coverage but not systematic public logging.[[8]](https://www.nytimes.com/athletic/6431783/2025/06/18/college-football-officiating-referees/)

**X (Twitter) activity**: Mostly anecdotal complaints, isolated stats (e.g., Josh Pate noting Ohio State last in opponent penalization), or unrelated tracking (WNBA officials). No prominent public DB-building projects or threads matching the query.[[9]](https://www.essentiallysports.com/ncaa-college-football-news-national-analyst-warns-about-biased-officiating-in-ryan-day-marcus-freemans-championship-face-off-with-data-evidence-josh-pate/)

In short, Brymer’s papers are the strongest statistical bias studies; CAI’s dashboard is the closest structured crew/penalty tracker; CFBD/cfbfastR + fan analyses fill gaps for custom work. A Covers-style public CFB referee ATS/crew DB does not appear to exist. Data access (play-by-play, penalties) is better than ever, so more projects could emerge.
